Some products do not fit a machine from the catalogue: an unusual format, a fragile material, an operation that could only be done by hand until now. In that case we build the machine ourselves: machines for handling, palletising, processing and assembly. We design it around your product and your process, so it does exactly what is needed and nothing superfluous.

## Designing a machine starts in your factory

We always start in the factory, not at the drawing board. We look at how the product moves through the line today, where it falters and what operators have to correct every day. From that comes a concept that we test with your team, before a single plate is cut. That is how we avoid over-engineering and build machines that are simple, and therefore reliable.

## Design, build and CE marking in-house

We do design, mechanics, controls, commissioning and [CE marking](/en/services/ce-marking-machine-safety/) in-house. That applies to a single workstation and to a complete assembly line. You get one point of contact, short lines of communication and a machine that fits in with what is already there. And because we also do maintenance and optimisation, we build from day one with the years ahead in mind: easy to access and quick to change over.

## Having a machine built in five steps

It usually concerns repetitive work. A machine takes that over, so your people can be put to work on more enjoyable tasks.

### 1. Getting to know each other and taking a good look

We come to you. That first visit is always free, as long as there is coffee. [Book the first visit](/en/contact/). We find out what is really needed. Which products are involved, and how many? What are the largest and smallest dimensions, and how many variants are there? Do you want one adjustable machine or a machine per product? Manual or automatic?

We also talk about the investment straight away. After that first visit we can usually tell you roughly what to expect. If we cannot yet, we say so honestly.

Then we make it concrete: production volumes, cycle times and the output you need. We record that in a specification document, before we design anything. The colleague who will work out the technical side of your project is involved from the start. Over time, they take over the technical contact.

### 2. Letting it sink in and whiteboarding

After the first visit we write a report and let it rest for a while. The best ideas often come in the shower or while walking the dog. Then we whiteboard, at our place and at yours. We also look at what is already on the market. If an existing machine works with a few modifications, you are finished sooner and it costs less.

We refine our first estimate at every step. We only build once you give the go-ahead.

### 3. Building

Where possible, we build the machine in our own workshop. A modification or a section of a production line is built on site at your premises.

We keep going until we deliver what we agreed together. If we discover halfway through that something is missing, we find a solution together.

### 4. Testing and handover

First we test the machine at our workshop, then in production at your site. You can also have both tests done at your site. We install and fine-tune on request. If we build the whole system, we deliver it with CE marking. For a partial system we supply a declaration of incorporation. Where needed, we train your operators and maintenance team.

### 5. After handover

In an SLA (Service Level Agreement) we set out what you can expect from us for maintenance and breakdowns.

## How long does it take to build a machine?

On average it takes about six months from the first visit to a running machine. A simple machine or a modified existing machine can be quicker. A large engineering project usually takes longer.

## Rebuilding and relocating, even without drawings

Not every project starts from a blank sheet. We often rebuild, relocate machines or modify existing installations that we were not involved in from the drawing stage. Not everything is documented in those cases, and we solve things as we encounter them: on the job, on the shop floor.
